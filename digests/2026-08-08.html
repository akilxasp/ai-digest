Deep Dive
CONTROL MOVES DOWN THE STACK
AI companies are no longer content to rent compute or ship features on top of someone else's platform — Anthropic's new chip team and Claude Code's push toward autonomous action both bet on owning more of the stack, from silicon to decision-making. That same instinct shows up bottom-up: this week's best-loved community builds weren't flashy demos but engineers squeezing huge models onto phones and laptop GPUs, chasing the same efficiency Anthropic wants from custom silicon. Enterprises, meanwhile, are building governance layers like Rippling's spend console because autonomy and efficiency both raise the same question: who actually knows what the AI is doing and costing.

Daily Tip
If you're switching to an agent harness with auto-approve permissions, like Claude Code's new auto mode, scope its access before you turn it on rather than after — a dedicated project directory or sandboxed profile, not your whole home folder. Autonomy is only as safe as the boundary you set before the first task runs.
